# Selling Newsletter Sponsorships

Load this skill when the thing being sold is ad space in a newsletter
(`skills: [newsletter_sponsorship]` in harvey.yaml). The "product" is the
audience, and the prospect is a brand's marketing team.

## Who you are writing to

- **Who decides:** at small and mid-size consumer brands, the Head of Growth,
  Growth Marketing Manager, Partnerships Manager, or Influencer/Creator
  Partnerships lead. At a founder-led brand under ~20 people, the founder.
  Avoid generic inboxes (info@, support@) when a named partnerships contact
  exists.
- **What they care about:** cost per acquisition, not impressions. They are
  comparing your newsletter with Meta ads, podcasts and other newsletters.
  They will ask about open rate, click rate, audience fit and past partners.
- **What they fear:** paying for a placement that sends no buyers, an audience
  that doesn't match, and a creator who rewrites their claims.

## The signal that matters most

`SPONSORS_NEWSLETTER` means the brand already paid for a placement in a peer
newsletter. Use it as the observation in email 1, plainly and accurately:

- Good: "Saw Seed in Gut Weekly last week. Our readers ask about probiotics more
  than anything else."
- Bad: "I noticed you're investing heavily in newsletter marketing." (vague,
  and it overstates what you know)

Never claim to know how a placement performed. Never name a peer newsletter's
rates. If the brand appeared more than once (`SPONSOR_PLACEMENTS` ≥ 2), you can
say they "keep showing up" in newsletters like yours. Re-booking is a sign the
channel works for them.

## What to put in front of them (email 2 or the reply)

One fact at a time, from the product info in harvey.yaml, never invented:

1. Audience in one line: who reads it, and how many.
2. One engagement number: average open rate, or replies per issue.
3. The fit: why their product matters to these readers specifically.
4. The offer: a test placement, one primary slot, priced.

A first placement at an introductory price, with the brand's own tracking link
or code, lowers the risk enough that most brands will try it.

## Objections and how to answer them

| They say | Answer with |
|---|---|
| "Send me your media kit" | Send it, plus one line on why this issue's topic fits them. Then propose a date. |
| "What are your open/click rates?" | The real numbers from the product info. Open rate first, then clicks. If clicks are modest, mention replies or poll responses. |
| "Too expensive" / "No budget this quarter" | Offer one secondary placement or a test, or ask when next quarter's budget opens and follow up then. |
| "We work through an agency / marketplace" | Ask for the agency contact, or offer to list on the marketplace they use. |
| "We only pay per acquisition / affiliate" | Say whether a hybrid works (a lower flat fee plus a per-sale bonus), but only if the product info allows it. |
| "Not a fit for our audience" | Ask which audience they are going after. If it doesn't match, thank them and stop. |
| "Already booked" | Ask when their next booking window opens. |

## Rules specific to this offer

- **Accurate audience claims only.** Use the subscriber count, open rate and
  demographics exactly as given. No rounding up, no "fast-growing" unless it is
  true right now.
- **Don't repeat health, finance or results claims as your own.** In a health
  or wellness newsletter, the sponsor's claims stay in the sponsor's copy, and
  the placement is labelled as sponsored.
- **No disguised ads.** Offer the sponsored label (FTC requirement in the US)
  rather than an editorial-looking mention without disclosure.
- **One brand per category per issue** is a reasonable promise to make. Never
  promise exclusivity you haven't agreed.
- **Booking is the close.** The goal is a date in the calendar and a paid
  invoice, not a call. When they say yes, confirm the date, the placement, the
  price, what they need to send (copy, image, link, code), and the deadline.
